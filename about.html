I've been writing software since I was nine. What started as curiosity became the thing I'm best at — and the way I tend to look at most problems: what's the simplest system that makes this work better?

The turn towards business software came from real work. In warehouse and grocery-store jobs I kept running into slow, outdated processes — tasks done by hand that a bit of software could handle in seconds. So I started building tools to fix them, and found that's the work I care about most.

I'm now in my second year of a Bachelor of Computer Science at the University of Queensland, which keeps the fundamentals sharp while I build real things for real businesses. My strongest areas are Python, databases, AI workflow automation, and working with large datasets — and I build web, Java and Electron interfaces where they fit.

However the work is framed — a custom business system, a website, an onsite support visit — the approach is the same: understand the actual problem, be honest about what's worth building, and stay patient, practical and reasonable. I'm based in Brisbane and available for new projects and technology-support bookings.

How I work

Four things
that don't change.

Whether it's a business system or helping someone get their laptop working, these four things stay constant.

Direct

You work with me, not an agency. The person you talk to is the person who builds and supports the work — no hand-offs, no account managers relaying messages.

Honest

If custom software isn't the right call, I'll say so. Sometimes an existing product is the better answer, and I'd rather point you the right way than sell you something you don't need.

Practical

I care about what actually helps day to day, not features for their own sake. The right answer is usually simpler than it first appears.

Patient

Whether it's code or a confusing computer, I explain things plainly and take the time. There are no silly questions — only things that haven't been explained well enough yet.
